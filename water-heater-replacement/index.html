A water heater that is leaking through the tank body cannot be patched, and a cold house with a flooded utility closet is not a place to shop slowly. Emergency water heater replacement in Holly Springs starts with a call to a dispatcher who is on duty 24/7. A licensed plumber then confirms the tank has failed, quotes the new unit and the labor, and tells you the price before work starts.

Before anyone arrives, you can limit the damage with four short steps, which are listed below. They take about five minutes and can save a floor.

A flooding tank that cannot be saved

Most heater problems are fixable. These are the ones that are not, because the failure is in the steel tank that holds the water.

Water seeping from the bottom of the jacket

If the leak is not from a pipe, valve or fitting, the inner tank has rusted through. Water pools under the unit and keeps coming even with the burner or element off.

A drain pan or floor that stays wet

A steady puddle that refills after you wipe it points to the tank. A once-only drip from the relief valve line is usually something else.

Rust-brown hot water with a metal smell

Hot water that runs brown while the cold tap stays clear suggests the tank lining and anode rod are spent. It often comes before the leak.

Repeated repairs and a unit in its second decade

A tank that has already needed a thermostat, element and relief valve in short order is close to the end. The next part cost goes toward a unit that is failing anyway.

Containing a leaking tank until help arrives

Do these in order. They protect your floor and your electrical or gas system.

  1. 1

    Close the cold water inlet

    Turn the valve on the pipe feeding the top of the tank clockwise. If it is stuck or missing, close the main shutoff where water enters the home.

  2. 2

    Kill the heat source

    On an electric unit, switch off its dedicated breaker. On a gas unit, turn the control knob to off. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 instead.

  3. 3

    Relieve the pressure

    Open a hot tap upstairs so the tank can drain slowly rather than push water out through the leak.

  4. 4

    Protect what is nearby

    Lift boxes and drywall-sensitive items off the floor, set a bucket under steady drips and keep children and pets away from standing water, especially around electric units.

Tank, tankless or heat pump: choosing the new unit

The right choice depends on your fuel, your space and how your household uses hot water. A same-day swap usually means a like-for-like tank, because changing type can add venting, electrical or gas work.

Standard tank, gas or electricFast like-for-like swap when the old tank failedUses the existing fuel, connections and often the same footprint

Sizing the replacement the right way

An undersized tank runs out in the middle of a shower, and an oversized one burns fuel heating water nobody uses. We start with how many people live in the house, how many bathrooms you run at the same time and whether you have a large soaking tub or a second-story laundry.

The first-hour rating on the yellow EnergyGuide label is more useful than the gallon number alone. It tells you how much hot water the heater delivers in a busy hour. If the old unit was always short, we say so and discuss a larger one, and if it was always more than enough, we do not have to match it.

Physical fit matters too. Ceiling height, doorway width and the space around the unit all decide whether a tall or a wide model is possible.

What a same-day swap looks like

Same-day appointments are available when schedules allow, and the work follows the same sequence each time.

  1. 1

    Confirm the diagnosis and the model

    We check the failure, read the data plate and agree on the replacement, fuel type and size before anything comes out.

  2. 2

    Drain and disconnect

    We shut off water and fuel, drain the old tank to a floor drain or outside, and disconnect the supply lines, the flue or vent and the gas or electrical hookup.

  3. 3

    Set and connect the new unit

    New flexible connectors, a fresh relief valve and discharge pipe, and a drain pan where the location calls for one. Gas connections are leak-tested before we leave.

  4. 4

    Fill, test and show you

    We purge air, check for leaks under pressure, set the thermostat, confirm the vent draws properly and show you the shutoffs and reset points.

Patching an old tank versus replacing it

A repair is the right call when a cheap part has failed on a sound tank. When the tank itself is leaking, the question is different.

Keep patching the old tank

  • Nothing seals the inside of a rusted tank
  • Costs you again when the next part or the tank itself fails
  • Risks water damage while the leak grows
  • Leaves you with an older, less efficient unit

Replace the heater

  • Gives you a new tank with a fresh relief valve and connectors
  • Lets you correct sizing, venting and drain pan problems
  • Ends the cycle of stacked repairs
  • Opens the door to a tankless or heat pump option if the site supports it

Venting, gas connections and code readiness

A gas water heater must push exhaust out through a vent that rises steadily to the outside. A vent with sagging sections, loose joints or rust at the draft hood can let exhaust gases spill into the room. We check the existing vent against the manufacturer's instructions, and we replace parts that do not pass.

Replacement also means attention to a few things the old unit may have lacked, such as a properly routed relief valve discharge, a sediment trap on gas supply lines where required and a drain pan in finished spaces. If your home plumbing has a closed system that traps pressure, an expansion tank may also be needed. See water heater installation for more on these parts.

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Holly Springs at 770-345-5536 if your home is inside city limits.

In unincorporated areas, the Cherokee County Development Service Center at 770-721-7810 issues plumbing permits to state licensed technicians. Its rules allow emergency work, such as a broken water heater at night, if the permit is obtained as soon as possible and a final inspection follows.

Removing the old heater

A used 40- or 50-gallon tank is heavy and awkward, and many homeowners do not want it in the garage for a month. Ask dispatch whether removal of the old unit is part of the visit, and get the answer along with the price before work starts.

If the old heater sits in an attic or crawlspace, tell dispatch. Access changes the time and sometimes the crew size, and we would rather plan for it than surprise you.

What affects the price of a water heater replacement

You get a clear price before work starts. Insurance generally does not cover a heater that wore out, though a policy may cover sudden water damage from the leak itself. Check with your agent.

Type and size of the new heater

A standard tank costs less than a tankless or heat pump model, and capacity and brand change the number.

Fuel and connections

Switching between gas and electric, or between tank and tankless, can add gas line, electrical or venting work.

Condition of the surrounding setup

Corroded shutoff valves, unsafe vent runs and missing drain pans add parts and time.

Access and location

A garage floor is simple. An attic or tight closet takes more handling.

Permits and timing

Where a permit is needed, it is part of the job. After-hours work may be priced differently, and you hear the number first.

Want your real number? Tell dispatch the fuel type, the location of the heater and what you see. We will walk through the visit before anyone is sent.

Gas odor near the heater

If you smell gas, do not turn lights on or off and do not try to relight a pilot. Leave the building with everyone in it and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. We will schedule a licensed plumber after the utility clears the home, and we cannot promise a response time on a gas odor.

Replacement choices in Holly Springs homes

The Cherokee County Water and Sewerage Authority (CCWSA) reports that its water is very soft, at 16 mg/L as calcium carbonate. A tank that fails here has often done so from age and corrosion rather than from scale, so the best replacement question is how old the old unit was and where it leaked. Check your own utility's water report if your water is not from CCWSA.

Plan the choice around your house, not a brochure. Homes in Canton, Ball Ground and Holly Springs run on a mix of older and newer construction, and that decides venting and gas supply options. Water heater replacement questions

Do you handle water heater replacement emergencies at night or on weekends in Holly Springs?

Yes. Dispatch is open 24/7 for failed tanks and flooded utility closets. Describe the unit, the fuel type and where the water is coming from, and the dispatcher arranges the earliest appointment available. You hear the price before work starts.

My water heater is leaking. Do I need a replacement or a repair?

If water is escaping from the tank body, you need a replacement. If it comes from a valve, a fitting or the relief valve line, a repair may be enough. We inspect before we recommend either. See water heater repair.

How long will I be without hot water?

A standard like-for-like swap is usually a same-day job when the right unit is available. Changes to venting, gas lines or electrical service take longer, and we tell you before work begins.

How do I know what size to buy?

It depends on the number of people, the number of bathrooms and your habits. The first-hour rating on the unit's label is a better guide than gallons alone. We size it with you on the visit.

Will you take away the old water heater?

Ask dispatch when you call, and have the answer before work starts. Tell them if the heater is in an attic or crawlspace so the visit can be planned around the access.

Do I need a permit for a new water heater?

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Holly Springs at 770-345-5536 if you are inside city limits. In unincorporated areas, the Cherokee County Development Service Center at 770-721-7810 handles plumbing permits.
