Septic tank or public sewer: the question behind every drain call

Homes outside public sewer lines use septic systems, so the first job in a drain emergency is working out which kind you have. A septic tank problem is not cleared like a household clog, and running a cable down the wrong line can push solids toward the drain field. Septic questions go to the North Georgia Health District at 770-479-0444.

Clues you are on septic include a buried tank lid in the yard, a county record, or a water bill with no sewer charge. If you cannot tell, say so on the call. Our sewer line cleaning page covers the pipe between house and street or tank, which is a different job from pumping a tank.

A drain backing up at night: guess or check first

The first ten minutes decide whether a backup stays small. Here are two ways to spend them.

Keep running water and hope

  • Every shower and load of laundry adds to the backed-up line
  • Cleaners poured into a stopped drain sit in the pipe
  • Septic users risk flooding a tank that is already full
  • Dispatch knows nothing about the system when the plumber arrives

Stop water and make the call

  • Water use stops, so the problem stops growing
  • You tell dispatch which fixtures are slow and whether the house is on septic
  • The emergency crew brings the right equipment the first time
  • You hear the price before any work starts

How the seasons strain Hickory Flat plumbing

A hard freeze does not come every winter in north Georgia, but when one arrives, outdoor spigots, crawlspace lines and attic water heater supplies are the first things to split. In the fall, shut off the outside valve and open the spigot, and a cold-snap leak becomes a lot less likely. If a pipe does burst, our burst pipe repair page walks through the repair.

Summer brings guests, extra laundry and irrigation, which push drains and septic tanks to their limits. Fall is when a failing water heater tends to show itself, as the first cold showers arrive. Whatever the season, the emergency line takes the same call at any hour.

Growth, permits and who to ask

Hickory Flat has seen rapid development this century, with new retail rising beside much older community buildings. Hickory Flat Academy dates to 1838 and Hickory Flat Elementary to 1903, so buildings here span nearly two centuries of plumbing practice. That mix is why we ask about pipe age at the start of a call.

The community is unincorporated, so plumbing permits go through the Cherokee County Development Service Center at 770-721-7810, not a city. Permits are issued to state licensed technicians, and the county allows emergency work such as a water heater failing at night. The permit must then be obtained as soon as possible, followed by a final inspection. Georgia's minimum plumbing code is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with state amendments.

Before the plumber reaches Hickory Flat

For a burst pipe or active leak, these steps buy time while the 24/7 line works on getting a plumber to you.

  1. Find and close the main valve

    Look where the line enters the house, often a basement wall, crawlspace or garage. Turn it fully closed, then open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the pipes.

  2. Take power out of the picture

    Switch off breakers for any room with standing water, including the water heater circuit. If the panel itself is wet or the floor around it is flooded, do not touch it and tell dispatch.

  3. Make room to work

    Pull furniture and stored boxes away from the wet wall, and lay towels to slow the spread. Park so a service vehicle can pull in close along the highway or your own drive.

  4. Call the emergency line

    Say whether the home uses septic or public sewer, which room is affected and what is running. If GPS gets confused between Hickory Flat Highway and a side road, give the nearest crossroad.
