Repairing a Broken Garbage Disposal in Holly Springs, GA

A humming disposal is usually jammed. A silent one is usually an electrical problem. A wet cabinet means a leak. Here is how to tell, and what to try safely.

  • By the Holly Springs Express Plumbing team
  • Published October 17, 2026
  • 5 min read

Short answer: if the disposal hums but the blades do not turn, it is jammed, and you can often free it in ten minutes with a hex key. If it makes no sound at all, check the reset button and the breaker. If water is collecting under the sink, a seal or connection is leaking, and a leaking housing means replacement.

Most disposals fail for one of three reasons, a jam, a dead motor or a leak. Each has a different fix, and mixing them up is how people waste a Saturday. This guide walks through the diagnosis, the safe steps and the point where replacing the unit makes more sense than repairing it.

Jam, dead motor or leak: what each one looks like

The symptom tells you where to start. Do not skip this part, since the fixes are not interchangeable.

Hum with no spinning

The motor is trying to turn the blades and something is stuck. This is a jam, most often a bottle cap, a fruit pit, a bone or a piece of silverware.

Silence when you flip the switch

No hum means no power is reaching the motor. A tripped reset button on the bottom of the unit, a tripped breaker or a loose plug are the usual causes.

Spins but drains slowly

The blades work and the problem sits downstream, often in the drain line or the trap. Our drain cleaning page covers that case.

Water under the sink

Look at the top flange, the dishwasher hose and the side of the housing. A drip from a hose clamp is a simple fix. A leak from the body is not.

How to free a jammed disposal safely

Never put your hand in the disposal, even when it is off. Use tools.

  1. 1

    Cut the power

    Switch off the wall switch, then unplug the unit under the sink or flip its breaker. Switch off is not safe enough on its own.

  2. 2

    Turn the blades from below

    Insert a quarter inch hex key into the socket in the center of the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth until the blades turn freely.

  3. 3

    Remove what is stuck

    Shine a flashlight down the drain and pull out the jam with tongs or pliers, never fingers.

  4. 4

    Reset and test

    Press the red reset button on the bottom, restore power and run cold water while you switch it on. If it trips again, stop.

Disposals, septic tanks and sewer rules in Holly Springs

If your Holly Springs home is on a septic system, ask the North Georgia Health District at 770-479-0444 before you lean on a disposal. A disposal sends more solids into the tank, and the health district can tell you whether it suits your system. Until you hear back, scrape plates into the trash instead.

If your home is on public sewer, ask your sewer provider about grease rules. Even with a disposal, grease belongs in a can and then in the trash, never down the drain. The same habit protects your own drain line, which is the pipe you pay to clear.

Repair the unit or replace it

A cheap fix is still wasted if the motor is on its way out. Use this as a guide.

Replace the disposal when

  • The body or housing is leaking
  • The motor is silent after a reset and a known good circuit
  • It trips the reset button repeatedly
  • The unit is old and has been repaired more than once

Repair the disposal when

  • The problem is a jam you can clear
  • A hose, clamp or flange gasket is the leak source
  • The motor spins freely and runs without tripping
  • The sink and plumbing around it are otherwise sound

What should never go in a garbage disposal

Most disposal damage comes from the same short list of items.

  • Grease, oil and fat, which cool and coat the drain line
  • Coffee grounds, which pack into the trap
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell into paste
  • Bones, fruit pits and corn husks
  • Eggshells and fibrous vegetables like celery and artichoke
  • Chemical drain cleaners, which can damage seals and burn skin

When to call a licensed plumber

Call if you smell burning, see sparks, or find water near the electrical connection. Replacing a disposal means wiring or a plug, a new mounting ring and a leak-free drain connection, and a mistake can flood the cabinet. Garbage disposal questions

Why does my garbage disposal hum but not spin?

Something is jamming the blades. Turn off the power, free the blades with a hex key from below, and pull out the object with tongs.

What does the reset button do?

It is a thermal breaker. When the motor overheats or strains, it pops out. Wait a few minutes, press it back in and try again. If it keeps tripping, the motor or a jam is the cause.

Can I run a disposal with a small leak?

Not for long. Water in the cabinet damages wood and can reach the electrical connection. Turn it off and have it checked.

Is it better to repair or replace an old disposal?

If the problem is a jam, clamp or gasket, repair makes sense. If the body leaks or the motor is dead, replace it.

Can I use a garbage disposal if my home is on a septic system?

Ask the North Georgia Health District at 770-479-0444 first. A disposal adds solids to the tank, so the district can tell you if it is wise for your setup. If your home is on public sewer, ask your sewer provider about grease rules.
