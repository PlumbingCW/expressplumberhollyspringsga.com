Snaking a Clogged Drain in Holly Springs, GA

A drain snake is the right first tool for a soft clog in one fixture. Here is how it works, where it fails and when to hand the job to a plumber.

  • By the Holly Springs Express Plumbing team
  • Published October 17, 2026
  • 5 min read

Short answer: a hand drain snake can clear a hair clog or a soft blockage in a single sink, tub or shower. It will not fix a grease-lined pipe, tree roots or a backup that hits several fixtures at once. If the snake stops against something solid or the problem comes back within days, stop and call a licensed plumber.

A snake is a coiled steel cable with a tip that hooks or cuts. You feed it into the drain, turn it, and either break up the clog or pull it back out. Used gently it is a reasonable DIY tool. Forced, it can scratch pipe, cut a hole in a trap or push the clog into a place where it is harder to reach.

Hand auger, drum machine and cable machine compared

The word snake covers three very different tools. Size and power decide what each one is safe to use on.

ToolTypical useReachMain limit
Hand auger (closet or sink auger)Toilet bowls, sink traps, tub drainsSeveral feetWeak against thick buildup or roots
Drum machine (small powered)Branch lines of one to two inchesA few dozen feetCan kink or bind in tight bends
Cable machine (larger powered)Main lines and larger drains with cutter headsA hundred feet or moreNeeds a cleanout and training to use safely

Using a hand auger on a sink or tub

Go slowly. A snake that is rushed is the one that jams or scratches.

  1. 1

    Clear the area

    Remove the stopper or strainer, set a bucket under the trap if you can reach it, and wear gloves and eye protection.

  2. 2

    Feed the cable in

    Push the tip into the drain until you feel resistance. Tighten the thumbscrew so about a foot of cable sticks out of the housing.

  3. 3

    Crank and push gently

    Turn the handle while you press the cable forward. The tip should bite into the clog or hook hair. Do not force a cable that will not move.

  4. 4

    Pull the clog out

    Retract the cable slowly, wipe it as it comes, and dispose of what you find in the trash. Run hot water for a minute to confirm the drain moves.

Who to call in Holly Springs when the clog is not in your house line

If the backup is in the public sewer main and not in your own line, your sewer provider is the right call. What a DIY snake can and cannot do

A hand snake is built for a narrow set of jobs. Past that, a plumber with a larger machine or a different method is the better choice.

A DIY snake cannot reliably

  • Scrub grease from the pipe walls, since it only drills a channel
  • Clear roots in an outdoor line
  • Reach a main sewer cleanout from inside the house
  • Tell you what is wrong with the pipe it is working in

A plumber's cable machine and camera can

  • Use cutter heads sized to the pipe
  • Reach long runs through a cleanout
  • Show the cause on camera when clogs keep returning
  • Confirm flow before leaving

Signs it is time to stop snaking

Many clogs get worse because someone kept pushing. Stop and call if any of these show up.

The cable stops hard against something

A solid stop can be a bend, a broken joint or a lump of roots. Cranking harder risks cutting into pipe or tangling the cable.

More than one fixture is slow

Several drains reacting together points to the main line, which a household auger cannot reach.

Water or sewage comes up elsewhere

A floor drain or a tub filling when you flush is a sign of a blockage downstream.

The clog returns within days

A snake opens a hole and leaves the coating behind. Coating that builds back quickly needs scrubbing, not another pass.

How a snake can damage a drain

Old galvanized or cast iron drains have rough, rusted walls. A cable can catch on a rusted joint, break off a flake of pipe or leave a spiral scratch that catches future debris. Plastic traps can crack if the cable is forced around a tight bend.

Powered machines are riskier. A drum machine can whip, tangle or kink if the cable binds, and a cutter head left spinning in one spot can chew through a thin pipe. That is why we recommend leaving powered work to someone who does it every day.

Chemical cleaners make it worse. If you poured one in earlier, tell the person who opens the drain, and wear eye protection.

When jetting or a camera is the better choice

If the same drain clogs every few weeks, a snake is solving the symptom. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe wall, which is the better answer for grease and heavy buildup. A sewer camera inspection shows what is actually in the pipe, such as roots, a belly or a cracked joint.

Drain snake questions

Can I use a drain snake on a toilet?

Use a closet auger, which has a protective sleeve to avoid scratching the porcelain. A standard drain snake can chip the bowl. If the closet auger does not clear it, stop and call.

How far down a drain can a hand snake reach?

Usually a few feet, enough for a trap and a short branch. A clog farther along needs a longer cable and often a cleanout access.

Is it safe to snake after using chemical drain cleaner?

Be careful. Chemicals can sit in the pipe and splash back when you open it. Wear eye protection and gloves, and tell any plumber what you used and when.

Why does my drain clog again after I snake it?

A snake clears a path through the middle and leaves buildup on the walls. The next clog forms faster. Jetting or a camera check is the usual next step.

Who do I call if the backup is in the public sewer main?

Your sewer provider. CCWSA lists 770-479-1813 in business hours and 770-479-2911 after hours for water customers, but check your bill, since we cannot confirm which agency serves each address. Septic homes call the North Georgia Health District at 770-479-0444.
