Tankless Water Heater Cost in Holly Springs, GA

The heater is only part of the bill. Gas piping, venting, electrical work and the age of your house often move the total more than the unit itself.

  • By the Holly Springs Express Plumbing team
  • Published October 17, 2026
  • 5 min read

We do not print a price range here because the total depends on your house, and a number that ignores your gas line or vent path would mislead you. What we can do is show what moves the cost, how to read a quote line by line and how to judge payback without made-up savings.

In short, a tankless install costs more than a like-for-like tank swap because the heater needs different gas supply, venting and often a new power connection. Whether that extra spend pays back depends on how much hot water you use and how long you stay in the house.

Gas line and venting: the two biggest unknowns

A tankless burner fires much harder than a tank burner for a short time, so the gas pipe feeding it has to deliver more volume. In some houses the existing line is large enough. In others a section must be upsized, which adds labor and sometimes a trip behind finished walls.

Venting is the other big item. Many tankless units need a specific vent material and a sealed run to the outside, and the manufacturer's manual sets the length and bends allowed. A heater that replaces a tank in the same spot can reuse an old flue only if the manual allows it, which is rare.

Water chemistry for Holly Springs tankless owners

If your water comes from CCWSA, the utility says Cherokee County water is very soft, measured at 16 mg/L as calcium carbonate. Soft water means scale builds up more slowly than in a hard-water region, but a heat exchanger still needs periodic flushing, and the manufacturer's manual sets the schedule. That is why flush valves belong on the quote even here.

The CCWSA annual water report is published for the public. Read it to see what is in your tap water, and call the utility if you are unsure whether your address is on its system.

How to think about payback without made-up numbers

Tankless heaters save energy mainly by not keeping a full tank hot all day. How much that saves depends on your usage, your fuel and the unit's efficiency rating, which the manufacturer lists on its spec sheet. Ask for the rating and compare it with your current heater's label.

A household that is away most of the day gains less than a busy household, and a house you plan to sell soon gains less than one you will keep. The honest test is simple: if the install premium is large and your usage is modest, the payback may be slow or may never come. Our tankless water heater page covers the models we install.

What a good quote should list

Compare these items across any two quotes before you compare totals.

  • The make, model and capacity of the heater, and whether it is gas or electric.
  • Gas line work, including any resizing, and the shutoff valve.
  • The vent material, run and wall or roof penetration.
  • Electrical work, condensate drain, and any permit the installer will pull.
  • Isolation or flush valves on the water lines for later descaling.
  • Disposal of the old heater and any wall repair.
  • A clear price before work starts, with changes agreed in advance.

A simple swap versus a full retrofit

Two homes can ask for the same heater and get very different totals.

Straight replacement of a tank

  • New unit goes where the old tank stood
  • Existing flue and gas pipe are reused when allowed
  • Less opening of walls and fewer trades
  • Often the faster, lower-cost path

Tankless retrofit

  • May need a new vent route and larger gas pipe
  • May add power, condensate drain and flush valves
  • Can free floor space and give endless hot water
  • Takes planning, and the quote has more lines

When tankless is the wrong choice

If the house cannot get an adequate gas supply or a legal vent path without major demolition, a tank may serve you better. Very small households with modest hot water use may never recoup the extra cost.

Insurance usually does not pay to upgrade a working heater, and it does not cover wear. If a failed tank caused water damage, ask your agent what the policy covers. For a standard swap, see water heater installation.

Tankless cost questions

Why do tankless quotes vary so much between companies?

Because the heater is only one line. Gas pipe sizing, vent routes, electrical work, wall repair and flush valves differ from house to house, and some companies leave items out of the first number. Compare the same list on each quote.

Do I need a bigger gas line for a tankless heater?

Often, though not always. The unit's input rating decides, and the installer should check your pipe size against the manual. If the line is too small, the heater will not reach full output.

How do I know if a tankless heater will pay for itself?

Compare the extra install cost with the energy savings listed on the spec sheet and your own hot water use. The longer you stay and the more hot water you use, the better the case.

Does tankless need maintenance?

Yes. Manufacturers call for periodic flushing to remove scale, and flush valves make that simple. Skipping it can shorten the unit's life and may affect warranty terms, so read the manual.

If my water comes from CCWSA, do I still need to flush a tankless heater?

Yes, follow the manual. CCWSA describes Cherokee County water as very soft at 16 mg/L as calcium carbonate, so scale is less of a worry than in hard-water areas, but the maintenance schedule still comes from the manufacturer.
