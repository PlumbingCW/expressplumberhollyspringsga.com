Water Heater Thermocouple Replacement in Holly Springs, GA

If the pilot lights but will not stay lit, a worn thermocouple is the most common reason. Here is how to confirm it, what to leave to a licensed plumber and when the heater itself is the problem.

  • By the Holly Springs Express Plumbing team
  • Published October 17, 2026
  • 5 min read

A thermocouple is a small heat sensor beside the pilot flame on a gas water heater. When the pilot stays lit, it produces a tiny electric current that tells the gas valve to stay open, and when the flame goes out, the current stops and the gas shuts off. If your pilot lights while you hold the button but dies the moment you let go, a worn or dirty thermocouple is the most likely cause.

Replacing one is a short job on many older heaters. It is not a job to try if you smell gas, and it is not the answer on every heater, because newer models often use electronic ignition with no standing pilot at all.

Gas smell comes first

If you smell gas near the heater, leave the building right away. Do not flip switches or use a phone inside. Call the gas utility or 911 from outside, and call a plumber only after the gas company says the house is safe.

What the thermocouple does and how it fails

The sensor tip sits in the pilot flame. Heat on the tip creates a small voltage, and that voltage holds a magnet inside the gas control valve open. No flame means no voltage, and the valve closes. It is a simple safety device, and it is built to fail shut.

Over years the tip can burn thin, collect soot or bend away from the flame. A dirty pilot orifice, a sideways draft or a weak magnet in the gas valve can look the same from outside. That is why the first step is a diagnosis, not a part swap.

Signs that point to the thermocouple

These symptoms are typical of a failing sensor, though none of them proves it alone.

The pilot goes out when you release the button

You hold the knob down, the flame stays, and it dies a few seconds after you let go. The sensor is not generating enough current to hold the valve.

The pilot flame looks weak or yellow

A healthy pilot burns blue and wraps the tip. A lazy yellow flame can mean a dirty orifice, which affects both the sensor and the burner.

No hot water after a windy day or a draft

If the flame blows out and will not relight, check for drafts around the vent and base before blaming the part.

The heater has relit several times in a month

Repeated outages suggest a deteriorating sensor, a failing gas valve or a venting issue, not bad luck.

What a homeowner can check, and where to stop

Reading the label and looking is safe. Opening gas fittings is not.

  • Read the lighting instructions on the heater label, and follow the manufacturer's instructions exactly.
  • Confirm the gas shutoff on the supply pipe is open and the heater control is not set to off.
  • Check that the heater is gas. Electric models have no thermocouple.
  • Look at the pilot flame once it is lit: blue and steady is healthy, yellow or flickering is not.
  • Stop if the flame will not stay, if you see soot or scorch marks, or if anything smells like gas.
  • Leave the gas control valve, burner assembly and gas fittings to a licensed plumber.

Replace the sensor or the larger part

A thermocouple is a small, simple part, so it is the right first guess only when the symptoms fit.

Swapping only the thermocouple

  • Fits the symptom of a pilot that dies on release
  • Short job on a standing-pilot heater
  • Fails again if the real fault is the gas valve
  • Does nothing for a tank that is rusted or leaking

Replacing the gas valve or the heater

  • Needed when a new sensor does not hold
  • Makes sense when the tank is old, rusted or leaking
  • Lets you move to a model with electronic ignition
  • Costs more up front but ends repeat visits

Thermocouple and pilot questions

How do I know if my water heater uses a thermocouple?

Look for a small pilot flame you light by hand, usually behind a small window or access panel at the base. Heaters with push-button electronic ignition or a display often have no standing pilot. The label and manual say which type you have.

Is it safe to replace a thermocouple myself?

It involves a gas connection on many heaters, so we advise leaving it to a licensed plumber. If you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Why does my pilot light go out again after a new thermocouple?

A new sensor will not help if the gas valve magnet is weak, the pilot orifice is dirty or a draft blows the flame out. A second failure calls for a valve check and a look at the venting.

Should I replace a thermocouple or the whole water heater?

Compare the heater's age and condition with the repair. A sound tank with a bad sensor is worth fixing. A rusted or leaking tank is not, and our plumber can tell you which you have.

Inside the city limits, ask City Hall at 770-345-5536. In unincorporated Cherokee County, the Development Service Center at 770-721-7810 allows emergency work such as a broken water heater at night, with the permit obtained afterward and a final inspection.
