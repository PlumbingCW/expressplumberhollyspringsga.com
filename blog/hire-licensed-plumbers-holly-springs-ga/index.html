How to Hire a Licensed Plumber in Holly Springs, GA

Look up the license, get the quote in writing and ask the right questions before anyone touches a pipe. Here is the checklist, including for emergencies.

  • By the Holly Springs Express Plumbing team
  • Published October 17, 2026
  • 5 min read

Short answer: before you hire, ask for the plumber's Georgia license, look it up yourself, and get the scope and price in writing. A real licensed plumber will not mind any of those steps. A plumber who dodges them is telling you something.

This matters most when you are stressed, since a burst pipe at midnight is when people agree to anything. The steps below take a few minutes and apply to any company, including us. We are licensed and insured, and we give a clear price before work starts.

How to verify a plumber in four steps

Do this before you sign anything, even when the job feels urgent.

  1. 1

    Ask for the license information

    Ask the plumber or company for the name on the license and the license type. A legitimate business answers right away.

  2. 2

    Look it up on your own

    Use the state's online license lookup instead of trusting a card or a text message. Search by the name or business and confirm the status.

  3. 3

    Match the details

    Check that the name on the license matches the name on the quote, the truck and the invoice. Check the license type fits the work.

  4. 4

    Confirm insurance

    Ask for proof of liability insurance, and make sure the policy is current.

Red flags before you hire

Most bad plumbing jobs show warning signs at the first call.

No license information offered

Vague answers, a different name on the paperwork, or reluctance to let you look it up are reasons to move on.

A price that changes at the door

A low phone price that jumps once the plumber arrives is a classic bait tactic.

Pressure to decide now

A real emergency still leaves time to read a one-page quote.

Cash only and no paperwork

No invoice and no receipt means no record if something fails.

Demands full payment up front

Large deposits before parts arrive are a warning, especially from someone you cannot contact later.

Licensing and permits in Holly Springs

Georgia licenses master and journeyman plumbers through the Georgia Secretary of State's Construction Industry Licensing Board. You can check a license using the Secretary of State's online license lookup. Do it before the plumber starts, and match the name to the quote and the truck.

Permits depend on your address. Inside Holly Springs, call City Hall at 770-345-5536. In unincorporated areas, Cherokee County issues plumbing permits only to state-licensed technicians, so a plumber who cannot show a license cannot pull one. The Cherokee County Development Service Center is at 770-721-7810.

The state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. That is the standard a permitted job is held to, which is another reason to ask who pulls the permit.

What a written quote should list

A good quote is specific. It lets you compare two companies on the same terms.

  • The business name, address and contact details on the document
  • The exact work to be done, in plain language
  • Parts and materials, with brand or type where it matters
  • Labor, listed separately from parts
  • Any after-hours, trip or diagnostic charge
  • What happens if the plumber finds a bigger problem
  • Permit handling, and who is responsible for it
  • Warranty terms on parts and labor, written out

Questions to ask and what a good answer sounds like

You do not need to ask all of these. Pick the ones that match the job.

Avoiding emergency price gouging

When a pipe is flooding the kitchen, the fear of paying more makes people stop comparing. Two minutes of questions still fits. Ask for the price before the plumber starts, and ask whether it is a flat figure or an open-ended hourly charge.

Shut off the water at the main valve first so you are not racing a clock. That lets you wait for a clear quote instead of agreeing to the first number. Be cautious of anyone who arrives unannounced after a storm and offers to start immediately.

Licensed plumber versus an unlicensed handyman

A handyman can be good at many things. Plumbing that connects to the water supply or the sewer is a different category.

Unlicensed work often means

  • No permit, so no inspection of the finished job
  • Little recourse if a joint fails behind the wall
  • Insurers questioning damage from unpermitted work
  • Trouble when you sell the house

A licensed and insured plumber offers

  • A state license you can look up
  • Work done to the plumbing code that applies
  • Permit handling when permits are required
  • A written quote and a point of contact if something fails

What a permit tells you about the job

A plumbing permit means the work is recorded, and in many cases it triggers an inspection. That second set of eyes is why a permit is a sign of a careful contractor. Ask whether your job needs one, who pulls it and who pays for it.

Hiring a plumber questions

How do I check if a plumber is licensed in Georgia?

Use the Secretary of State's online license lookup and search by name or business. Confirm the status and the license type, and match the name to your quote.

Should I get more than one quote?

For planned work, yes. Compare scope, parts, labor and warranty, not just the total. For an emergency, at least ask for a price before the plumber starts.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work?

It depends on the job and the address. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Holly Springs at 770-345-5536 for city addresses and the Cherokee County Development Service Center at 770-721-7810 for unincorporated areas.

Who issues plumbing permits if I live outside the city limits?

In unincorporated Cherokee County, the Development Service Center issues them, and only to state-licensed technicians. Call 770-721-7810. Inside Holly Springs, ask City Hall at 770-345-5536.

What does licensed and insured mean?

Licensed means the plumber holds a state license you can look up. Insured means liability coverage in case of damage on the job. Ask to see both.
