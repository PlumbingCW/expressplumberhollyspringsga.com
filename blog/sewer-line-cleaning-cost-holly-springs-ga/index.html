Sewer Line Cleaning Cost in Holly Springs, GA: What Sets the Price

No honest price exists before someone sees your line. This guide shows what moves the number and how to read a quote.

  • By the Holly Springs Express Plumbing team
  • Published October 17, 2026
  • 5 min read

The price of sewer line cleaning depends mostly on four things: whether there is a cleanout to work from, how long the line is, what is blocking it, and whether a camera is needed to see the cause. We give a clear price before work starts, and we do not name a figure before we know which of those apply to your house.

If waste is backing up into a floor drain or tub right now, call dispatch, which is open 24/7. Otherwise read on for the cost factors, the cable versus jetting choice and how to keep the line clear.

What moves the price of cleaning a main line

Use the table to understand a quote. Each row shows what makes the job quicker or slower.

Cost factorEasier jobHarder job
Cleanout accessA cleanout outside the house at ground levelNo cleanout, so the plumber must pull a toilet or open the line
Line lengthA short run from house to the public connectionA long run, or one with several branches
Cause of the clogSoft buildup, paper or greaseThick tree roots or scale on the pipe wall
Equipment neededA standard cable machineA larger cutter head or a jetting unit
Camera inspectionNot needed, the line clears and flowsNeeded to find a break, belly or root source
TimingA scheduled visit during normal hoursAn urgent visit when the line has backed up

Signs your main sewer line needs cleaning

A main line clog behaves differently from a sink clog. Watch for these patterns.

The lowest drain backs up first

A tub or floor drain on the lowest level gurgles or fills when you run the washer or flush a toilet upstairs.

More than one fixture is slow

Several drains slowing at once, in different rooms, points past any single trap.

Sewage smell indoors or in the yard

A foul smell near a cleanout or a soggy patch of lawn can mean the line is backing up or leaking.

Repeat clogs after a normal cleaning

If the same line clogs again within weeks, something is growing back or the pipe has a defect.

Cable cleaning versus hydro jetting

Both clear a line, but they work differently, so the right choice depends on what is blocking the pipe.

Cable machine

  • Spins a cutting head through the clog to open a path
  • Good for paper, soft buildup and many root intrusions
  • Often quicker to set up and suited to a first visit
  • May leave a coating on the pipe walls that regrows

Hydro jetting

  • Scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water
  • Removes grease, scale and fine roots more completely
  • Needs a pipe sturdy enough for the pressure
  • Usually costs more than a basic cable visit

Sewer service and septic in Holly Springs

The Cherokee County Water and Sewerage Authority (CCWSA) runs three wastewater treatment plants, named Rose Creek, Fitzgerald Creek and Riverbend. We cannot tell you which one serves a given street. Ask your sewer provider where the public main ends and your responsibility starts, because that boundary decides who fixes a blockage past the cleanout.

Not every home is on public sewer. If yours uses a septic system, call the North Georgia Health District at 770-479-0444 instead, since a septic tank problem is handled differently from a clogged house line. Tell dispatch when you call so the visit starts with the right question.

How a main line cleaning visit runs

Knowing the order helps you spot a rushed job.

  1. 1

    Find the access point

    The plumber locates the cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the house, or decides where else to enter the line.

  2. 2

    Clear the blockage

    A cable machine cuts through the clog. If roots or grease coat the wall, the plumber may switch to a larger head or jetting.

  3. 3

    Look inside with a camera when needed

    A camera shows whether the clog came from roots, a broken joint or a low spot. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what you receive.

  4. 4

    Confirm flow and explain what was found

    Water is run through the line to confirm it drains freely, and you hear what likely caused the clog and what comes next.

What a sewer cleaning quote should list

Ask for these items in writing before you agree.

  • Where the plumber will enter the line and whether a new cleanout is part of the job.
  • Which method is planned, cable or jetting, and why.
  • Whether a camera inspection is included or priced separately.
  • What happens if roots are found, and whether that changes the price.
  • Whether the job covers the whole line or only the section behind the blockage.
  • A clear price before work starts, with anything that could change it spelled out.

Insurance, upkeep and honest limits

Homeowners insurance usually does not pay to clear a clog caused by roots or buildup. Some policies pay for water damage when a sudden backup floods the house, so call your agent before the cleanup starts and take photos.

Cleaning is maintenance, not a repair. If the camera shows a cracked or offset pipe, clearing it only buys time and sewer line repair is the real fix. For lines that clog on a schedule, hydro jetting and regular checks cost less than repeated emergencies.

Keep wipes, grease and paper towels out of the drains, and avoid planting trees directly over the line. A camera look every so often shows roots before they block. For smaller blockages, see drain cleaning.

Sewer line cleaning questions

Why can't you give a price for sewer cleaning over the phone?

Access, line length, clog cause and equipment all change the work, and most of them are unknown until someone looks. We give a clear price before work starts, after we see the line.

How often should a main sewer line be cleaned?

Only when it shows symptoms or a camera finds buildup or roots. Lines with heavy tree growth nearby may need regular checks, but there is no fixed schedule that suits every house.

Is a cleanout necessary?

It is not required to clear a line, but it makes the work faster and cleaner. Without one, the plumber may need to pull a toilet or open the line another way, which can add time.

Will cleaning stop tree roots for good?

No. Cutting roots clears the pipe but does not kill the tree or seal the joint they entered through. Roots tend to return unless the joint is repaired or the line is lined or replaced.

Who do I call about a septic system in Holly Springs?

Septic questions go to the North Georgia Health District at 770-479-0444. A septic tank is not cleared like a house sewer line, so tell dispatch if your home is not on public sewer.
