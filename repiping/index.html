A repipe replaces the water supply pipes that run through your walls, attic and crawlspace, and it is worth doing when the old pipe is failing faster than you can patch it. Whole-house repiping in Holly Springs starts with a licensed plumber looking at what you have, explaining the options and giving you the price before work starts. Dispatch is open 24/7, so you can call about an assessment whenever it suits you.

Most people reach this page after a second or third leak, brown water at the tap or a plumber who said the pipe is at the end of its life. The goal here is to help you decide if a repipe is the right move or if something smaller will do.

Signs your supply pipes are wearing out

No single sign proves a repipe is needed. A pattern of several is what points that way.

Repeated leaks in different spots

One leak is a repair. A leak every year or two in different rooms means the pipe is thin throughout, and each patch only moves the pressure to the next weak point.

Rusty or brown water that does not clear

Galvanized steel rusts from the inside. CCWSA notes that water standing in galvanized service lines or interior plumbing can dissolve iron, which shows up as discolored water after the house sits idle.

Weak flow that keeps getting worse

Mineral and rust buildup narrows old pipe, so a shower that has slowly weakened over the years may be a pipe problem and not a faucet one.

Green stains, pinholes or flaking on copper

Blue-green crust on fittings and tiny weeping holes along the pipe show corrosion working from the water side.

Pipe material known to fail

Some materials, such as polybutylene, have a history of failure. If a plumber identifies them in your walls, plan a replacement before it fails.

Galvanized pipe, polybutylene and old copper

Galvanized steel was common in older homes and is the most frequent reason for repiping. It rusts from the inside, so the pipe may look fine outside while the opening shrinks. Replacing galvanized pipe is usually a full replacement because patching a section leaves the rest corroding.

Polybutylene is a gray or blue plastic pipe used in many homes for a period and later linked to failures at fittings. If a plumber confirms it in your house, a repipe replaces the lines and the fittings. Old copper is different. If it is in good condition with only a joint failing, a repair may be enough, but pinholes in several places suggest the copper is worn through.

Not sure what you have? A plumber can tell by looking at the pipe near the water heater, under sinks and at the main valve.

How a repipe is staged to keep your house usable

A whole-house repipe sounds like a disruption, and it is, but a good plan keeps it contained.

  1. 1

    Walk the house and plan the route

    We look at every fixture, find where the main enters, note attic and crawlspace access and decide where new lines can run with the fewest openings.

  2. 2

    Set up and protect the house

    Floors get covered, furniture is moved and the water shuts off only when we are ready to cut over.

  3. 3

    Run new lines before cutting the old ones

    In many houses the new pipe is installed alongside the old, which limits the time the water is off to the cutover.

  4. 4

    Connect, test and restore

    Each fixture is connected, the system is pressurized and checked for seepage, and we turn the water back on with you present.

  5. 5

    Close up and walk through

    We show you what changed. Drywall patching is separate, and we tell you up front what falls on the plumber and what belongs to a drywall contractor.

A partial repipe versus a whole-house repipe

You do not always need the whole house done at once. The question is whether the rest of the pipe is healthy.

Partial repipe

  • Replaces one wing, floor or branch with new pipe
  • Costs less up front and is less disruptive
  • Makes sense when the failures are limited to one run
  • Leaves the old pipe elsewhere, which may fail later

Whole-house repipe

  • Replaces supply pipes throughout
  • Ends the cycle of repeat leaks
  • Makes sense when pipe is failing in several places
  • Handles the walls once instead of in stages

Drywall, patching and what to expect inside the walls

Many repipes open small access holes in drywall, closets and ceilings, and some houses can be done mostly through the attic or crawlspace. Where walls are opened, we cut neat, square openings so they are easier to patch.

We handle the plumbing. Drywall finishing, texture and paint are normally done by a drywall or paint contractor, and we tell you before the job begins so you can line that up. It is wise to ask for photos of the openings before they are closed.

What affects the price of a repipe

We give a clear price before work starts. A repipe is a custom job, so there is no honest flat number until someone looks at the house. Homeowner insurance usually does not pay to replace pipe that is simply old, though it may cover water damage if a pipe fails suddenly. Ask your agent.

Size and layout of the house

Access and finishes

The more tile, plaster and built-ins in the way, the more careful work it takes.

Before your repipe day

A little preparation makes the work smoother.

  • Clear the areas around sinks, water heater, closets and attic access.
  • Fill containers with drinking water and plan where family members will wash while the supply is off.
  • Tell us about pets, home offices and anyone who needs water at certain times.
  • Check your homeowner policy and take photos of the existing pipes and walls.
  • Ask us which permit applies and who will arrange the inspection.

Repiping, permits and older homes in Holly Springs

Georgia's minimum plumbing code is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with state amendments, and a repipe is normally permitted work. Repiping questions

How do I know if my house needs a repipe?

Look for a pattern. Leaks in several places, rusty water that persists, weak pressure at many fixtures and a pipe material with a known failure history all point toward a repipe. A plumber can inspect the pipes and tell you whether repair is enough.

How long will my water be off during a repipe?

It depends on the house, but we plan the work so the water is off only during the cutover instead of the whole job. We explain the schedule before we begin.

Which is better, PEX or copper?

Neither wins in every house. PEX is faster to install and flexible in tight walls. Copper handles heat and sunlight and has a longer track record. We talk through your layout and budget.

Will repiping damage my walls?

Some openings are normal, but we plan the route to keep them small and tidy. Drywall patching and paint are usually separate work.

Do I need a permit to repipe my house?

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Holly Springs at 770-345-5536, or the Cherokee County Development Service Center at 770-721-7810 for unincorporated addresses. We tell you which applies before work begins.

My home was built before 1992. Is there lead in my pipes?

CCWSA says homes built before 1992 in Cherokee County could have some lead plumbing components, such as fixtures or solder. A water test tells you what reaches your tap, and a repipe is a good time to replace old fittings.
