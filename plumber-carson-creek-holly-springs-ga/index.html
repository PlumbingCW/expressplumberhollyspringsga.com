A water bill that jumps with no obvious cause

In a larger home, a slow leak can run for weeks before anyone sees it. These are the signs worth checking first.

The meter keeps moving with everything off

Turn off every tap and appliance, then watch the meter dial. If it moves, water is going somewhere. Leak detection finds where without tearing into walls first.

A toilet that runs without a flush

Drop a little food coloring in the tank. If color shows in the bowl without flushing, the flapper is leaking. It is a quick fix, but it can add a lot to a bill.

Damp or warm spots on a floor

A warm patch on a slab floor can point to a hot water line leak. See slab leak repair for how that is found and fixed.

An irrigation zone that pools

A sprinkler line break outside is usually not an indoor plumbing job. If the water is coming from a house supply line, call us.

How a leak check works in a bigger house

The plumber starts with the meter, then isolates parts of the house with the shutoffs. That narrows the search to one branch of supply pipe before any equipment comes out.

If the leak is hidden, the plumber works section by section until the spot is found. Our water line repair page explains what repair follows once it is found.

What to have ready when you call

  • Your street address, since some listings show a Canton mailing city
  • A rough picture of where the water, damp spot or odor is
  • Whether you already closed the main shutoff or a fixture valve
  • Roughly how old the water heater is, and whether it is gas or electric
  • Any recent plumbing work or changes to the home
